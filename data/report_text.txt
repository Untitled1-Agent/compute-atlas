Google leads the comparable modeled fleet. The Q4 2025 chip-owner model estimates 5.04M H100e and 2.947 GW of accelerator TDP, implying approximately 4.72 GW IT under the central fleet bridge. Microsoft follows at 3.50M H100e, Amazon at 2.46M and Meta at 2.42M. [S04,S05; Model]
The named-site database is a floor. It covers 86 campuses, 13.3 GW IT and 14.6M H100e, but estimates only 46% coverage of deployed global AI compute at end-June 2026. [S01,S02]
A complete GB200-class IT GW costs $37.883B upfront and $8.514B/year in the common model. Company-linked evidence spans Meta facility assets at $5.4B/IT-GW, Amazon campus infrastructure at $7.81B/IT-GW after IT-power normalization, a Google-backed facility at $9B-$11B/IT-GW, Microsoft GPU hardware at $29B/IT-GW, CoreWeave powered-shell leases at $1.44B-$2.34B/IT-GW-year, and full GPU service near $9.7B-$10.9B/IT-GW-year. These are different layers, not an additive stack. [S03,S17,S24,S26,S28,S29,S33,S34,S37,S39-S46,S49; Model]
Signed and contracted demand is larger than recognized assets. Amazon/Anthropic announced more than $100B over ten years and up to 5 GW; Oracle/OpenAI announced 4.5 GW additional; Google described a 5 GW TPU program from 2027; CoreWeave disclosed 3.7 GW contracted. [S21,S22,S24,S33,S38]
The footnotes are the real capacity map. Microsoft disclosed $329.1B of mostly data-center uncommenced leases, Meta $346.99B post-period, Oracle $260B and CoreWeave $35.5B. These figures are not directly additive with capex or purchase obligations. [S12,S27,S31,S37]
Equity exposure bifurcates. Google has the best modeled compute-to-valuation combination among hyperscalers; Oracle and CoreWeave offer greater AI-capacity torque but carry much larger obligations relative to market capitalization. [S31,S37,S38,S48; Model]

Contents and source conventions




1. Measurement framework
1.1 There is no single meaningful “gigawatt”
Accelerator TDP is the summed design power of chips. IT load includes host CPUs, memory, networking and storage. Facility load adds cooling, pumps, power conversion and redundancy. Utility or campus capacity can include spare blocks and non-AI loads. Treating these as interchangeable can overstate or understate comparable compute by 20%-100%. [S01,S02,S05-S11]

For fleet estimates based on accelerator TDP, the central bridge is facility power = 2.0x accelerator TDP and IT power = facility power / 1.25 PUE = 1.6x TDP. Sensitivity ranges are 1.7x-2.3x for facility/TDP and 1.18x-1.35x for PUE. [S05; Model]
1.2 H100-equivalent normalization


1.3 Counting and uncertainty rules
Current named-site capacity is counted once at the attributed vendor roll-up, including dedicated third-party supply where the customer is named. [S01]
Fleet chip-owner estimates are not added to named sites. Reconciled current compute takes the maximum of the site floor, chip-owner model and later direct disclosure. [S01,S04,S05; Model]
Target-state sites are separate from signed contracts. A target may lack binding customer demand; a contract may be served across multiple sites. [S16-S34]
The site database estimates 46% coverage and reports material uncertainty. This report uses floors and ranges rather than false precision. [S01,S02]

4.1 Amazon / Anthropic
Anthropic and Amazon announced an expansion above $100B over ten years for up to 5 GW of new AWS capacity. Nearly 1 GW of Trainium2/Trainium3 capacity is expected by end-2026, and Anthropic stated that more than one million Trainium2 chips were already in use. Project Rainier was fully operational in June 2025 with nearly 500,000 Trainium2 chips and an expected expansion above one million. New Carlisle is the second-largest tracked live AI site at 910 MW IT. [S01,S25]
4.2 Google / Anthropic
Anthropic announced up to one million Google TPUs, tens of billions of dollars of services and well over 1 GW online in 2026. A separate announcement describes a 5 GW Google/Broadcom program beginning in 2027. Because overlap is undisclosed, this report shows 1 GW as a milestone and 5 GW as the program envelope rather than summing them. [S21,S22]
4.3 Microsoft: demand versus supply
Microsoft and Anthropic announced a $30B Azure purchase commitment and up to 1 GW of additional capacity. Separately, Microsoft contracted 200 MW IT from IREN for $9.7B over five years, approximately 200,000 GB300 GPUs from Nscale, and a $17.4B five-year GPU-services agreement with Nebius whose MW is undisclosed. Applying Nscale Texas disclosure of 240 MW for 104,000 GPUs to the total GPU count implies approximately 462 MW IT; a 700 MW option is excluded. [S16-S19; Model]

4.4 Oracle / OpenAI and CoreWeave
OpenAI announced 4.5 GW of additional Oracle capacity, bringing the Oracle-linked program above 5 GW under development and above two million chips. A linked announcement referenced more than $300B and more than 5.5 GW; dividing those values produces a rough $10.9B/GW-year over five years, but it is not a clean tariff. [S33,S34; Model]
CoreWeave disclosed 1.5 GW active and 3.7 GW contracted, so the simple incremental bridge is 2.2 GW. Its $104B backlog divided by contracted capacity is $28.1B/GW, but duration and service mix vary. [S38; Model]

6. Vendor dossiers
6.1 Amazon / AWS

AWS has the strongest vertically integrated named anchor: customer, ten-year minimum value, up-to-5-GW envelope and in-house Trainium roadmap. Trainium is approximately 41% of modeled Amazon H100e. [S04,S05,S24,S25]
The cost is cash intensity. First-half cash PPE exceeded OCF by 38%, and AWS added $90.120B of net PPE in six months. The Indiana program is $6.25B per disclosed data-center/facility GW and approximately $7.81B per modeled IT GW at 1.25 PUE. It is campus/infrastructure scope, not a clean full-stack accelerator bill. [S23,S26; Model]

6.2 Microsoft

Microsoft combines a large owned fleet with a broad outsourced network. IREN, Nscale and Nebius shift some construction, power and hardware financing to suppliers while reserving capacity for Azure. [S13-S18]
The benefit is speed and diversification; the cost is margin sharing and a complex stack. Microsoft had $329.1B of mostly data-center uncommenced leases and $743.821B of obligations, alongside $684B of RPO. [S12]

6.3 Alphabet / Google

Google is the compute leader under both the chip-owner model and the site floor. Approximately 76% of modeled H100e comes from TPUs, creating the strongest custom-silicon position. H100e does not capture TPU workload fit or fungibility. [S01,S04,S05]
Alphabet has $122.814B of assets not in service, versus $247.177B of technical infrastructure in service. Cloud RPO is $513.9B, while the $811B broad commitment total makes the forward envelope less transparent. [S20]
The Hut 8 River Bend filing supplies a cleaner, though indirect, denominator: $9M-$11M per contracted IT MW for facility construction and a $7.0B 15-year base lease on 245 MW of IT capacity, equal to approximately $1.90B/IT-GW-year. A Google subsidiary backstops the contract; Fluidstack is the tenant, so this is not Alphabet-owned capex. [S49; Model]

6.4 Meta

Meta monetizes compute through recommendation, advertising and model capability rather than external cloud contracts. There is no independently priced tariff to validate unit economics. [S27,S28]
The balance-sheet strategy mixes direct construction, leases and Blue Owl JV financing. The raw $27B / 5 GW = $5.4B/GW ratio covers long-lived facilities, not servers, and scope/timing are mismatched. [S28,S29; Model]

6.5 Oracle

Oracle has the most concentrated public AI demand bet. The OpenAI program adds 4.5 GW and a linked >$300B commitment, creating exceptional visibility and exceptional sensitivity to execution and financing. [S31-S34]
FY2026 capex was 174% of OCF. Customer prepayments and debt funded the build, while $260B of uncommenced data-center leases equals approximately 60% of market cap. [S31,S48; Model]

6.6 CoreWeave

CoreWeave is the purest public compute exposure; its landlord network reveals powered-shell pricing that hyperscaler filings obscure. [S37-S46]
Debt and uncommenced leases each equal roughly three-quarters of market cap; net PPE approximates market cap; and three customers represented 72% of Q2 revenue. Short hardware life amplifies utilization and renewal risk. [S37,S38,S48; Model]

6.7 Tesla

Tesla compute supports autonomy, robotics and model development; it is not sold as general-purpose cloud. Q2 company-wide capex of $5.789B exceeded OCF of $4.697B, but compute is only one capital use. [S47]
At $37.883B/GW, 205 MW implies approximately $7.8B of replacement capex, below 1% of market cap. A 400 MW target is approximately $15.2B, around 1.2% of market cap. [S03,S47,S48; Model]

7.4 Per-company cost per comparable IT GW

The comparable engineering lens holds installed cost per IT watt constant at the Epoch base case—$37.883B upfront and $8.514B/year per IT GW—and applies each company's reconciled H100e density. The observed lens uses project, lease, hardware or service transactions and retains each transaction's scope. [S02,S03; Model]

8.1 Published base case

Epoch AI one-GW GB200-class model [S03]. Five-year IT life and 14-year facility life.
Servers and network are 69% of upfront cost and about 73% of annualized TCO. Extending useful IT life from three to seven years lowers annual TCO from approximately $12B to $7B/GW; hardware refresh dominates land and power. [S03]

Raising electricity from $0.05 to $0.20/kWh increases annual TCO by approximately $1.12B/GW at 71% utilization and 1.20 PUE. Changing IT asset life from seven to three years changes annual TCO by approximately $5B. [S03; Model]

10. Risks, uncertainty and what would change the conclusions

The most useful future disclosures would be accelerator and IT MW by generation; active versus reserved capacity; lease starts by site; customer ramp and utilization; power pass-through; refresh responsibility; and cloud gross margin after depreciation and financing. Until then, ranges and layer-specific comparisons are more defensible than single-point fleet values.

Appendix A. Normalization detail

Formula: IT kW/GPU = system kW / GPUs per system x (1 + external overhead). Facility values divide by PUE. B100 power uses an explicit H100-compatible air-cooled approximation. [S05-S11; Model]
