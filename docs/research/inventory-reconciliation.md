# Reconcile inventories through reviewed links, not merged records

Research decision for [ticket #36](https://github.com/Untitled1-Agent/compute-atlas/issues/36), 11 October 2026. This note recommends an implementation boundary; it accepts no identity, geometry, ownership or capacity claim. Repository inspection used `6ce2cba67c67c892e88b03bef0367f7a386a7061`. First-party pages were checked on 11 October; their current content is not a replacement for the dated accepted collections.

## Decision

Keep every collection and its native identifiers intact. Connect records using a separate, versioned editorial link publication that states exactly which relationship was reviewed. A reviewed correspondence between two records does not certify a parcel, building outline, current operating state, owner, tenant or critical IT load. Those require their own dated, scoped evidence. Never promote a directory entry or community feature into an additional capacity project through matching.

Use the existing acquisition → candidate → named review → accepted publication pattern. Exact names, operator codes, addresses and source geometry can produce candidates; proximity is supporting context only. Do not silently choose the nearest outline, collapse overlapping map records, split compound directory entries or apply transitive identity across project/phase/building boundaries.

## Current matching and review workflow

The Equinix proposal function requires a full code token, an Equinix name/operator token and compatible country label. It aliases `USA`, `UK` and `UAE` solely for candidate generation. It does not truncate suffixes: `FR2.6` is not `FR2`, and `PA9X` is not `PA9`. On the actual accepted collections it returns 139 proposal edges for 130 directory codes: 122 have one candidate and eight have multiple candidates. All are still proposals, even the single candidates. [Server matching](../../server/operator_directory.py), [browser matching](../../src/directory-schema.js).

Digital Realty's browser offers the first four building-outline anchors within 250 m, sorted by distance. This is a capped geographic preview, not a match registry or an exhaustive candidate list. Distance is between source anchors, not necessarily entrances or nearest polygon edges. [Publisher explorer](../../src/publisher-explorer.js).

Four checked-in operator reviews corroborate named Equinix locations using code and address: PA9X, FR5, AM3 and LD8. They deliberately leave IT MW null and geometry unverified. These are useful seeds for a link-review migration, but accepting the directory publication alone must not accept every generated proposal. [Existing reviews](../../data/catalog/reviews.json).

The research identity API exposes effective accepted identity facts separately from historical anchors. Its typed contract supports campus name/locality with municipality, county/parish, region or country precision; coordinates remain `not_established`. The only typed inter-project relation currently supported there is source-cited `adjacent_project`. The ordinary relationship ledger is for a project and a company, not two inventory records. Do not smuggle cross-collection links into either schema as unconstrained text or stretch these campus facts into building certificates. [Identity contract](../../server/identity.py), [claim validation and decisions](../../server/store.py), [research scope validation](../../server/research.py).

Catalog and publisher acceptance are already separate, immutable histories with named actor/reason and an expected-current hash. Accepted snapshots, pending acquisitions and rejected/superseded claims must remain distinguishable. The new link publication should follow these semantics and add endpoint-version checks; refreshing any source must not silently re-accept its old links against changed content. [Catalog ledger](../../server/catalog.py), [directory ledger](../../server/operator_directory.py).

## Identity vocabulary and scope

| Role | Meaning | Allowed consequence of a reviewed link |
|---|---|---|
| Research project | A named development, contract or investigated campus scope | Find corroborating source records; no new quantity or automatic deduplication |
| Campus | A sourced geographic/operational grouping | Membership only when the source establishes it; no parcel outline inferred from a name |
| Phase | A source's delivery/development or lease tranche | Link to its parent project and its own scoped observations; not automatically a building |
| Publisher facility/code | The operator's service, location or marketing identity | Correspondence to a named facility at a reviewed address; not a unique physical-building count |
| Physical building | A specific physical structure established by evidence | Geometry or phase allocation only with explicit independent review |
| Source feature | An OSM element or interpreted imagery feature, with its own vintage | Inspect that source assertion; do not inherit project ownership, operating state or MW |

An address agreement can establish that two records describe the same named facility while leaving the physical outline uncertified. Shared postal addresses can serve several structures or tenants. An operator may develop a campus for a separate compute tenant; matching only a project's `primary_company` would miss that relationship or assign the tenant as owner. Preserve role-specific project/company evidence separately from native map `operator`, `owner` or `brand` tags.

Helios demonstrates the distinction: Galaxy reports delivery to CoreWeave under a lease and separately reports Phase I gross power and critical IT load. That establishes the named phase/tenant/developer relationship, not a capacity allocation to whichever roof or utility feature lies nearby. A historic aerial feature retains its imagery date; a 2026 phase statement does not make the outline a current survey. [Galaxy Phase I disclosure](https://www.galaxy.com/newsroom/galaxy-completes-phase-i-of-its-helios-data-center-campus), [accepted evidence](../../data/evidence.json), [campus scene provenance](../../data/campus/scenes.json).

## Editorial link publication contract

Implement one narrow, independently accepted link collection rather than a new global “unique facilities” total. A correspondence is many-to-many and versioned; a one-code/one-outline requirement would discard legitimate cases and conceal contradictions.

Each link proposal/revision needs:

- Stable link ID, schema version, relation type and typed endpoints: collection/publisher, native record ID, endpoint record hash and publication hash. OSM endpoints additionally retain element type/ID, source version and last mapped edit. Record hashes must use a specified canonical projection.
- `identity_scope` (`named_facility`, `named_project`, `campus_membership`, `phase_membership` or `physical_building`) and supported relation, such as `describes_same_named_facility`, `describes_same_named_project`, `part_of_campus`, `phase_of_project` or `adjacent_project`. Explicitly validate allowed endpoint-role pairs. `nearby_source_feature` remains geographic context, never identity.
- Supporting source IDs/URLs, exact locators (address section, named project paragraph, permit identifier, page/figure), source content identity if captured, source reporting date and date precision, capture UTC, review UTC and source rights. A URL alone is not a source version.
- Compared raw names/codes/addresses and locality scope; any normalization or operator-alias rule used for candidate discovery; contradiction flags and counterevidence. Keep source country group and reviewed geographic locality in separate fields.
- Candidate-generation method/version and, when relevant, distance plus `distance_basis=source_anchor_haversine`. Save all candidates and the total, not just the capped UI preview. Evidence or exact-code candidates must not be discarded merely because a coarse pin is outside 250 m.
- Explicit boundary flags: `geometry_verified=false` unless an independent geometry claim is reviewed; capacity allocation absent; owner/operator/tenant claims absent unless separately linked. A named-facility correspondence cannot implicitly set any of these flags true.
- Append-only review decision with actor, reason, status, decision time and `supersedes`; unresolved evidence remains a proposal. Rejecting a correspondence leaves both native records intact. A changed endpoint/source produces a review-needed revision, retaining the old decision against the old bytes.

For implementation, preserve the current internal editorial write boundary and read-only public APIs. A link head requires expected-current publication identity, validated endpoint publication identities, named actor and reason in one transaction. Candidate generation and background refresh must never move the accepted link head. An acquisition/parser acceptance and a link acceptance are separate decisions. These requirements extend the existing review pattern; they are not implemented in the current identity module.

Accepted read views should expose only effective accepted links. Candidate/rejected/history views must identify themselves separately. After a source refresh, show a previous link as “reviewed against earlier source version” rather than applying it to a changed record as current evidence. Geometry-only edits need an explicit impact policy: named-address correspondence may survive unchanged identity fields, but geometry verification cannot carry forward automatically. Record-level hashes avoid invalidating every link because an unrelated directory row changed; publication hashes still identify the full reviewed context.

## UI and export requirements

The detail rail should show collection role first (“research project”, “publisher location”, “community building outline”), then native code/ID and source vintage. Separate “Reviewed correspondence”, “Proposed identity candidates” and “Nearby source features” as user-visible states. A candidate count or “linked” color must not imply an accepted identity. Show conflicts beside the candidate, particularly PA7's incompatible address, compound titles and source geography inconsistencies.

When changing from a publisher pin to a community outline, preserve the selection/camera return route and explicitly switch the selected source identity. Show both coordinates with their bases, rather than replacing the publisher pin with the outline center. A retained historical project anchor can coexist with a reviewed preferred navigation anchor; any future preference is a distinct reviewed display decision with its own provenance, not a rewrite of archive coordinates.

Export the selected records in source-specific envelopes: collection ID, publication/capture identity, complete native fields, source URL, rights/attribution, original location basis, and unknown values as null. Include the independently reviewed link and its decision/evidence fields, any proposal status, selection filters and full-publication denominators. Preserve original code, source title, country heading, OSM tags, element version, geometry and relation membership. Do not flatten all sources into a single `operator` field, remove rejected correspondence history from audit exports or export illustrative heights/camera values as measured dimensions.

Project observations remain in their own evidence envelope with subject, scope, reporting date, boundary, unit, status and source. MW are never copied to linked map features or divided among outlines by count/area/proximity. Native tags and reviewed ownership appear side by side with separate dates and sources. The existing capacity ledger alone remains the source for accepted capacity measures.

Keep ODbL geographic records attributed and independently exportable. Source-specific operator factual projections retain their publisher rights labels; linking them does not make their pages or imagery ODbL. Preserve separate files/envelopes and review combined redistribution separately rather than relabeling a mixed database with one source's license. [OSM copyright/license](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), [existing source-rights research](2026-10-09-additional-operator-sources.md).

## Unresolved questions that do not block the boundary decision

- No reliable first-party physical-building inventory resolves ME4/FR7's multi-outline cases, the full JB1+JB3 compound footprint or Fairwater phase polygons in this research. Keep them unresolved rather than picking a polygon.
- HND10's current facility-page title differs from the accepted compound capture. A future bounded source acquisition must record and review the exact response and parser projection; this browse is not such an artifact.
- The SG4 facility page failed to load here. Shared community address/name is insufficient for a new first-party review decision.
- Define the source-change impact policy and controlled operator aliases before implementing renewal of accepted links. Acquisition/operator-corporate changes must not rewrite ownership or migrate native IDs automatically.
- A complete global unique-facility census would require a different evidence and coverage program. This decision supports traceable reconciliation and navigation without claiming that denominator.
